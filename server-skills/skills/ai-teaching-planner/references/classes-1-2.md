# Classes 1–2: Discovering Smart Machines
ELI level: Class 1 = ELI6, Class 2 = ELI7. Ages ~6–8.

## Topic list (sequence roughly in this order)
1. Machines around us (fans, toasters, washing machines)
2. Humans and machines — what each is good at
3. What makes a machine "seem smart"?
4. Giving clear instructions (the seed of "algorithm," never use that word)
5. Patterns, sorting, colours, sounds, shapes
6. Introduction to robots
7. Simple everyday examples of AI (voice assistant answering a question, a phone unlocking by face)
8. Safe use of technology (asking a grown-up, not sharing personal info)

## Calibration for this band
- **Language**: very short sentences, everyday words only, no jargon at all (not even simplified jargon — skip terms like "algorithm," "data," "AI" itself can be introduced late and softly, e.g. "smart helper machines").
- **Depth**: one idea per lesson, reinforced through repetition and play, not stacked concepts.
- **Examples**: things in the child's own home and classroom — fan, calculator, TV remote, a toy that lights up.
- **Lesson length**: short attention spans — favor 30–40 minute lessons with frequent activity switches (every 5–8 minutes).
- **Activities**: stories, games, pictures, drawing, sorting objects, role-play ("be the robot"), sound/shape matching, physical movement. No screens required.
- **Math**: none, or only counting/sorting (how many red blocks vs blue blocks).
- **Coding**: not compulsory. If used at all, only unplugged (physical direction cards, "if-then" as a simple game) — no actual programming interface.
- **Projects**: a single-session craft or role-play activity, not a multi-day project.
- **Assessment**: oral questions, thumbs up/down, matching pictures, simple show-and-tell — no written tests.
- **Responsible AI**: framed as simple safety rules and stories ("always ask a grown-up before talking to a machine that talks back," "machines can make mistakes too").

## Sample analogies that work at this level
- A smart machine is like a very obedient but literal-minded helper — it only does exactly what it's told.
- Sorting toys by color/shape is what a machine does with a "pattern."
- A robot vacuum "sees" the room the way a child with their eyes closed uses their hands to find the wall.

Avoid: probability, data privacy debates, technical vocabulary, screens-only lessons, anything requiring reading fluency as a bottleneck.
